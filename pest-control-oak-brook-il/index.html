Pest Control in Oak Brook, IL

Oak Brook is a village of about 8,000 people with more open space per home than most towns around it. Larger yards, Salt Creek and the oak woods of Fullersburg mean the outdoors is a big part of life here, and outdoor pests are a big part of pest control. We treat Oak Brook homes and yards together.

Room to Spread Out

Oak Brook was known as Fullersburg before it was incorporated as a village in 1958. It has far fewer people per square mile than its neighbors. In 2010 it had about 990 people per square mile, while nearby Westmont and Clarendon Hills each had over 4,000. That lower density often means larger lots, more lawn and landscaping, and more space between neighbors.

More yard means more surface for pests. Mosquitoes, ticks, earwigs and ants have more places to live, and treating a large property takes more than a quick spray at the door.

Salt Creek and Fullersburg Woods

Salt Creek runs through Oak Brook, and Fullersburg Woods Forest Preserve, with its oak woodlands, follows the creek. The preserve is also home to beavers, red foxes, herons, egrets and nesting songbirds. Historic Graue Mill, built in 1852, sits on the creek. Early settlers described the land along Salt Creek as damp, clay rich bottomland that drained poorly.

That mix of water, clay soil, shade and woods explains a lot. Damp ground helps mosquitoes breed. Wooded edges shelter ticks. Wildlife brings fleas and rodents close to yards, and wet, shaded landscaping is where earwigs and centipedes settle.

What Oak Brook Yards Deal With

  • Mosquitoes around creeks, low areas, shady shrubs and standing water
  • Ticks at wooded edges, in leaf litter and in tall grass
  • Rodents moving in from woods and fields in fall
  • Fleas carried into yards by passing wildlife
  • Earwigs in mulch beds, window wells and damp landscaping
  • Wasps, hornets and yellow jackets building in trees, shrubs, sheds and ground burrows

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are mosquitoes so bad in some Oak Brook yards?

Creeks, low spots, clay soil that drains slowly and shady shrubs all help mosquitoes breed and rest. Dumping standing water weekly and treating shaded resting areas cuts numbers. See Mosquito Control.

Do I need tick control if my yard backs onto woods?

It is a good idea. Ticks wait in leaf litter, brush and tall grass at wooded edges, and wildlife carries them into yards. Yard treatment focused on those edges lowers your risk. See Tick Control.

Can wildlife near Salt Creek bring pests to my home?

Yes. Wildlife can leave fleas and ticks in yards and push rodents toward homes in fall. Sealing gaps around your foundation and garage and treating your yard help.

How do you handle a large yard?

We walk the full property and focus on the areas where pests live and rest: shady beds, wooded edges, water spots and the foundation. We do not need to treat every inch of lawn.

What are earwigs and why are they in my landscaping?

Earwigs are harmless insects that hide in damp mulch, leaf litter and window wells by day and come out at night. Pulling mulch back from the foundation and reducing moisture helps. See Earwig Control.
