## Signs You Have Rats

* Droppings larger than mouse droppings, roughly the size of a large raisin
* Scratching, thumping or running sounds in the attic, ceiling or walls, mostly at night
* Burrow holes a few inches wide along the foundation, under decks, sheds or in the garden
* Greasy dark rub marks along walls, pipes and beams
* Large gnaw marks on wood, plastic, pipes or wires
* Torn garbage bags or pet food that goes missing
* Tracks or trails in dust, dirt or grass
* A strong musky smell in closed areas

## Where Rats Live Around Downers Grove Homes

The rat most people find in this area is the Norway rat. It prefers to live at ground level and digs burrows close to where it finds food.

Common spots around homes:

* Along the foundation and under concrete slabs
* Under decks, porches and sheds
* Near garbage cans, compost piles and dumpsters
* Around bird feeders and pet food bowls
* In dense bushes, ivy and woodpiles
* In basements, crawl spaces and garages

Older homes with foundation cracks and gaps around pipes give rats an easy way in. A rat can fit through a hole about the size of a quarter, so small openings still matter.

## Mistakes That Make Rat Problems Last

**Only using store bought traps.** Rats often avoid new objects. Traps placed in the wrong spot may never be touched.

**Using poison without a plan.** A rat that eats poison can die inside a wall or attic, which causes a strong smell. Poison can also harm pets and children.

**Sealing holes before the rats are gone.** This can trap rats inside your home and push them to chew new openings.

**Ignoring the yard.** If burrows, food and hiding spots remain outside, more rats will find their way in.

***

## Why You Should Not Wait

Rats cause damage fast. They chew wires, which can be a fire risk. They damage insulation, pipes and wood. They leave droppings and urine that can spread germs and set off allergies. They also breed quickly, so a few rats can become many.

***

## Tips to Help Prevent Rats

* Keep garbage cans closed and away from the house
* Store pet food and bird seed in sealed containers
* Clean up fallen fruit, nuts and pet waste in the yard
* Keep wood piles, bushes and clutter away from the foundation
* Fix leaks and remove standing water
* Repair gaps around pipes, vents and the foundation
* Do not compost meat or cooked food

**How do I know if I have rats and not mice?**
Rats are larger, and so are their droppings and gnaw marks. Droppings from rats are roughly the size of a large raisin, while mouse droppings look like grains of rice. Burrows along the foundation and greasy rub marks on walls also point to rats.

**How do rats get into a house?**
They use gaps around pipes, vents and garage doors, cracks in the foundation, and any opening about the size of a quarter. They can also chew through weak materials to make the gap bigger.
