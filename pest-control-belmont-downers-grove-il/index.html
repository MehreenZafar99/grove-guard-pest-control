An Established, Walkable Pocket

Belmont grew up around its own train stop, which made it an early, walkable extension of the village. That history shows in the housing stock: older homes with mature trees, established foundations and, in many cases, decades of small settling and wear. The Belmont Golf Club and its open turf sit nearby, which adds another edge of green space close to backyards.

Older single family homes in Belmont often have gaps that have opened up over the years around foundations, siding and utility lines, which is exactly what mice, ants and spiders use to get inside.

Mature trees along residential streets give carpenter ants a place to nest and give ticks and mosquitoes shelter close to yards.

Proximity to the golf club and open turf means more standing water after rain and more insects moving between the course and nearby yards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do older Belmont homes get more pests than newer construction?

Age brings small gaps around foundations, siding and utility openings that build up over decades. Those gaps are what mice, ants and spiders use to get indoors. See Rodent Control.

Does living near the golf club affect mosquitoes in my yard?

It can. Open turf and water features near a golf course can add to standing water and mosquito breeding nearby, especially after rain. See Mosquito Control.

Are carpenter ants a concern on tree lined Belmont streets?

Yes, especially around mature trees with dead limbs or damp wood. Carpenter ants often travel from a tree into a nearby house. See Carpenter Ant Control.

Should older Belmont homes get a termite inspection?

It is worth it, especially before buying or renovating. Older wood framing and foundations can be more susceptible. See Termite Inspection.
