## Spiders You May See in Downers Grove

Most spiders in this area are harmless to people. Knowing which one you have helps decide what to do.

**American house spiders.** Small, brown and the most common spider inside homes. They build tangled cobwebs in corners, ceilings, basements and garages.

**Wolf spiders.** Large, hairy and brown or gray. They do not build webs to catch prey. They hunt on the ground and often come inside in fall. They look scary but rarely bite.

**Cellar spiders.** Pale, with very long thin legs. They hang in loose webs in basements, crawl spaces and dark corners. Many people call them daddy long legs.

**Jumping spiders.** Small and compact, they hop and are often seen on sunny walls, windows and window frames.

**Funnel weavers.** Fast brown spiders that build flat webs with a funnel in grass, shrubs and along foundations, and sometimes wander inside.

**Black widows and brown recluses.** These spiders can bite in a way that needs medical care. Black widows do occur in Illinois but are not common around homes in the Chicago area. Brown recluses are mostly found in southern Illinois, and many spiders reported as brown recluses here turn out to be harmless. If you think you have found one, call us and keep a photo or the spider itself for identification.

## Why Spiders Come Into Your Home

Spiders follow their food. If your home has insects, it will have spiders.

* **Insects indoors and out.** Ants, flies, moths and other bugs draw spiders in
* **Outdoor lights.** Bright lights attract insects, and spiders build webs nearby
* **Gaps and cracks.** Spiders enter through small openings around doors, windows, vents and pipes
* **Clutter.** Boxes, stored items and piles in garages, basements and closets give spiders places to hide
* **Yard and landscape.** Woodpiles, thick shrubs, ivy, leaf litter and trees near the house give spiders shelter close to the walls

***

## When Spiders Are Most Noticeable

Spiders are around all year, but you are likely to see them most in late summer and fall. That is when many of them are fully grown and males wander in search of mates. Cooler weather also pushes some spiders, like wolf spiders, to move indoors.

In spring, spiders come out of hiding as insects become active, and you may see fresh webs on eaves, porches and around doors.

## What You Can Do at Home

* Switch outdoor lights to warm colored bulbs, which attract fewer insects than bright white ones
* Seal gaps around doors, windows, pipes and vents
* Move firewood and clutter away from the house
* Trim shrubs and branches so they do not touch the walls
* Clear webs with a vacuum or a long duster
* Reduce clutter in basements, garages and closets
* Keep window screens in good repair

***

## Mistakes That Bring Spiders Back

**Killing spiders one by one.** Store sprays only kill the spiders they touch, and new ones move in.

**Ignoring the insects.** Spiders will stay as long as there is food, so treating only the spiders is not enough.

**Cleaning webs but not the outside.** Webs come back quickly if the outside of the home is not treated.

Most spider bites are minor. If a bite causes severe pain, spreading redness, blistering or you feel unwell, seek medical care. We are a pest control company and cannot give medical advice.

**Why do I have so many spiders?**
Spiders go where the food is. A lot of spiders often means a lot of other insects. Outdoor lights, gaps and clutter also make your home a better place for them.
