## What Earwigs Look Like

The earwig most often found in Illinois is the European earwig.

* **Size:** About five eighths of an inch long
* **Color:** Reddish brown, with a flat, slim body
* **Pincers:** A pair of curved pincers at the tail end. The male's are more curved than the female's
* **Wings:** They have wings but rarely fly
* **Habits:** They come out at night and hide in dark, damp places by day

***

## The Earwig Myth

Earwigs do not crawl into people's ears to lay eggs or eat brains. That story is an old myth. The name comes from an old folk tale. Earwigs will sometimes pinch with their pincers if handled, which can be uncomfortable but is not dangerous.

## Why Earwigs Come Around Your Home

**They love moisture.** Earwigs need damp places, and they thrive in mulch, leaf litter, wet soil and dense plants.

**They hide near the house.** Mulch against the foundation, stacks of wood, stones, flower pots and clutter give them cover close to the walls.

**Weather pushes them in.** During hot, dry spells, earwigs go looking for water and may move indoors. After heavy rain, flooded hiding spots can also send them into houses.

**They are drawn to lights.** Outdoor lights attract the insects that earwigs eat and can draw earwigs too.

In Downers Grove, homes with landscaped beds, mulch, window wells and mature plantings around the foundation provide the kind of shelter earwigs like.

## What Earwigs Eat

Earwigs eat a mix of plants and other insects.

* Decaying plant material
* Aphids, mites and small insects, so they can be helpful in some gardens
* Soft plants, seedlings, flowers, and fruit like berries and peaches
* Crumbs and food in the home in rare cases

They cause damage in gardens when numbers are high, but they usually do not harm your house.

## What You Can Do at Home

* Pull mulch a few inches away from the foundation, and keep it thin
* Clear leaf litter, boards, stones and debris from around the house
* Store firewood away from the walls and off the ground
* Fix leaky faucets, hoses and irrigation
* Make sure gutters and downspouts drain away from the house
* Seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes
* Use a dehumidifier in damp basements
* Switch outdoor lights to warm colored bulbs, which attract fewer insects

**A simple garden trap:** Roll up a damp newspaper or put a shallow tin with a little vegetable oil near the plants at dusk. Earwigs hide in it overnight, and you can dispose of them in the morning.

## Earwig Season

Earwig numbers build through spring and early summer and are usually highest in mid to late summer. Hot, dry weather sends earwigs looking for water, and heavy rain pushes them from their hiding places. In fall, females dig nests in the soil to lay eggs and guard them through winter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are earwigs dangerous?**
No. They do not sting or spread disease. They can pinch if handled, but it is not serious.

**Do earwigs crawl into ears?**
No. This is a myth. Earwigs do not seek out human ears.

**Why do I have so many earwigs?**
Damp mulch, leaf litter, stacked wood and clutter give earwigs places to breed and hide. Hot, dry or wet weather can also draw more of them to your home.

**Will earwigs damage my garden?**
They can. In large numbers, they leave ragged holes in leaves and flowers and can harm seedlings and soft fruit. In small numbers, they also eat other insects, so they can be useful.

**How do I keep earwigs out of my house?**
Remove damp hiding spots near the foundation, seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes, and reduce moisture in basements. An exterior treatment helps cut down how many get close to the house.

**How long does it take to get rid of earwigs?**
Most homes see fewer earwigs within a couple of weeks after treatment, though moisture and hiding spots need to be dealt with to keep numbers down.

**Is earwig treatment safe for children and pets?**
Your technician follows the product label and tells you where products were applied and how long to keep children and pets away from treated areas.
