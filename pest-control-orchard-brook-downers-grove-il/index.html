Wooded Lots Near the Park

Doerhoefer Park sits at the center of this neighborhood, bringing wooded areas, open turf and drainage features close to nearby backyards. That combination of trees, shade and low spots that collect water shapes the pest pressure homes in Orchard Brook tend to see.

Wooded park edges next to residential yards give carpenter ants, ticks and spiders easy cover close to homes, especially where trees overhang fences or side yards.

Low lying areas that hold water after rain are common mosquito breeding spots, and standing water near a park or drainage area can affect yards several blocks away.

A mix of older homes and newer infill construction means pest pressure can vary a lot house to house, since older foundations and mature landscaping bring different entry points than a recently built home.

Frequently Asked Questions

My yard backs up to Doerhoefer Park. Does that mean more mosquitoes?

It can, especially if there are low spots or drainage areas nearby that hold water after rain. Standing water close to a park can affect several blocks of yards around it. See Mosquito Control.

Are ticks a real concern this close to wooded park land?

Yes. Ticks wait in shaded grass, brush and leaf litter along wooded edges, and yards that back up to park land see more exposure than yards further away. See Tick Control.

Why do carpenter ants show up near mature trees here?

Older or damaged trees along wooded lots give carpenter ants a place to nest, and they often travel from the tree into a nearby garage or house. See Carpenter Ant Control.

My house is newer construction, do I still need pest control here?

Yes. Newer homes in Orchard Brook still sit close to the same wooded, low lying park land, so mosquitoes, ants and spiders can move in from the surrounding landscape regardless of when the house was built.
