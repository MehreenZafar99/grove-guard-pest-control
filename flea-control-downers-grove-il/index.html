Flea Control in Downers Grove, IL

Itchy ankles, a scratching pet and tiny black specks on the carpet all point to fleas. Killing the fleas you see is not enough, because most of the problem is hidden in eggs and larvae. We treat the home and the yard to break the flea cycle.

Signs of Fleas

  • Pets scratching, licking or biting at their skin more than usual
  • Small red bites in clusters on ankles and lower legs
  • Flea dirt: black specks in pet bedding, on carpet or on your pet's skin. Place them on a wet white paper towel, and if they turn reddish brown, it is flea dirt
  • Tiny dark insects jumping on pets, carpets or your socks
  • Fleas that jump on white socks when you walk across a carpet

Why Fleas Are Hard to Get Rid of

Fleas have four life stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult. Adult fleas live on your pet, but most of the flea population is not on the pet at all.

  • Eggs fall off your pet into carpet, bedding, furniture and cracks in floors
  • Larvae hide deep in carpet fibers and dark spots away from light
  • Pupae sit in a protective cocoon that can wait for weeks or months until it senses warmth, vibration or carbon dioxide, which means someone is close by
  • New adults hatch and jump onto pets and people

That is why fleas can seem to come back suddenly after you have treated your pet, or when you return to a home that has been empty for a while.

Where Fleas Come From

  • Pets, both dogs and cats
  • Wildlife in the yard, such as raccoons, opossums and stray cats, which leave fleas under decks, porches and sheds
  • Visiting pets
  • Used furniture, rugs or bedding

Even pets that stay mostly indoors can pick up fleas when they go outside for a short time.

How We Get Rid of Fleas

Flea control works best when the home, the yard and the pet are all handled together.

1. Inspection. We check where your pets sleep and spend time, both indoors and outdoors.

2. Treat the home. We treat carpets, rugs, baseboards, furniture and other places where flea eggs and larvae collect.

3. Treat the yard. We treat shady spots where pets rest and where wildlife may leave fleas, such as under decks, along fences and in dense plants.

4. Follow up. New fleas continue to hatch after treatment, so a second visit about two weeks later helps catch them.

Your vet handles your pet. We do not treat pets. Talk to your veterinarian about a flea product for every dog and cat in the home. This step matters as much as treating the house.

How to Prepare for Flea Treatment

  • Vacuum carpets, rugs, furniture and floors well, and empty the vacuum outside in a sealed bag
  • Wash pet bedding, throw blankets and rugs on hot
  • Pick up items from the floor so we can reach the carpet and baseboards
  • Take pets out of treated areas during treatment and keep them out until your technician says it is safe
  • Keep vacuuming every day or two for a couple of weeks. The vibration helps hatch pupae so treatment can reach them

Mistakes That Let Fleas Come Back

Treating only the pet. Most fleas are in the home and yard, not on the animal.

Treating only the house. Fleas from the yard will come back in on your pet.

Using foggers. Foggers do not reach under furniture or deep in carpet, where larvae hide.

Stopping too soon. Pupae keep hatching for weeks. Skipping the follow up can bring the problem back.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I still have fleas after treating my pet?

The fleas on your pet are only a small part of the problem. Eggs, larvae and pupae in your home and yard keep producing new fleas. Treating the whole environment and your pet together is the way to stop the cycle.

How long does it take to get rid of fleas?

Most homes need a few weeks to be clear. Pupae can stay hidden and hatch over time, so you may still see some fleas after treatment. They should decrease steadily.

Can fleas live in my home without pets?

Yes, for a time. Fleas can arrive on wildlife, on visitors or in used items. They can also stay as pupae in an empty home and hatch when someone moves in.

Do fleas bite people?

Yes. Fleas bite humans, most often on the ankles and lower legs. The bites are itchy and can leave red spots.

Do I need to leave my house during flea treatment?

Your technician will tell you how long to stay out of treated areas and when it is safe for people and pets to return. Follow those directions closely.

Are flea treatments safe for pets and children?

Products are applied following the label. Your technician will explain where treatment was applied and how long to keep children and pets away. Talk to your vet about flea prevention for your pet.

How do I know if my pet has fleas or something else?

Look for flea dirt, part the fur near the tail and belly and check for small dark insects. Scratching can also come from allergies or other skin problems, so if you are unsure, ask your vet.
