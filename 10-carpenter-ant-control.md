## How to Tell Carpenter Ants From Other Ants

* **Size:** They are large, about a quarter to half an inch long, bigger than most ants you see in a kitchen
* **Color:** Black, or black with a reddish tint
* **Shape:** A narrow waist with one segment, a smooth rounded back and bent antennae
* **Activity:** Most active at night, so you may see them after dark near sinks, tubs and baseboards
* **Spring swarmers:** Winged carpenter ants appear in spring and can show up indoors

**Carpenter ants or termites?** Both can damage wood, but they are not the same. Termites are pale and thick waisted, and they build mud tubes. Carpenter ants leave clean, smooth tunnels and sawdust piles. If you are unsure, book a [Termite Inspection](/termite-inspection-downers-grove-il).

## Signs of a Carpenter Ant Nest

* Small piles of sawdust or wood shavings, sometimes mixed with bits of dead insects, below walls, window frames or baseboards
* A faint rustling or crackling sound in walls, especially at night
* Large ants seen indoors, particularly in kitchens and bathrooms
* Winged ants indoors in spring
* Ants traveling in lines along pipes, wires or the edges of walls
* Soft, damp or damaged wood near leaks or windows

Seeing a few carpenter ants now and then can mean they are only foraging. Seeing many, or seeing sawdust, usually means a nest close by.

***

## Where Carpenter Ants Nest

Carpenter ants usually keep a main colony outside, then send some ants to start smaller nests inside the home. These indoor nests often form in wood that is wet or damaged.

**Outside**
* Dead trees, stumps and hollow branches
* Wood piles and firewood
* Fence posts and old landscape timbers
* Tree branches touching the roof

**Inside your home**
* Window frames and sills with moisture
* Walls near bathrooms and kitchens
* Wood near roof leaks or under a damaged roof edge
* Around chimneys and skylights
* Decks, porches and door frames
* Hollow wood or insulation

Downers Grove has many older homes with mature trees right beside the house. That gives carpenter ants a source colony nearby and a path to the roof and siding. Carpenter ants are often active along tree lines in the area in spring.

## How Serious Is the Damage?

Carpenter ants usually cause damage more slowly than termites. But a colony that stays in a home for years can weaken wood, especially in areas that stay wet. The nest can also grow and spread into satellite nests in other parts of the house.

The sooner it is treated, the smaller the repair.

## What You Can Do at Home

* Repair roof, window and plumbing leaks
* Trim tree branches so they do not touch the house
* Remove stumps and dead wood near the home
* Store firewood away from the house and off the ground
* Seal gaps around pipes, wires and windows
* Keep gutters clean so water drains away from the walls

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do carpenter ants eat wood?**
No. They dig through it to make nests and push the sawdust out. They feed on other insects and sweet foods. The damage comes from their tunnels, not from eating.

**How do I find the nest?**
Look for sawdust piles, listen for rustling in walls at night and watch where ants go after dark. Because nests are usually hidden, a technician who knows where to look can find them faster.

**Are the winged ants in my house carpenter ants or termites?**
It can be hard to tell. Carpenter ants have bent antennae, a narrow waist and front wings longer than the back. Termites have straight antennae, a thick waist and wings of equal length. If you are not sure, call us for an inspection.

**Can I get rid of carpenter ants with spray?**
Spray may kill the ants you see, but it usually does not reach the nest, and the colony continues. Treating the nest and fixing the moisture that attracted them works better.

**How long does it take to get rid of carpenter ants?**
It depends on the size of the colony and how many nests there are. Many cases improve in a couple of weeks, but larger colonies can take longer. We explain what to expect at your visit. *(Confirm with renter.)*
