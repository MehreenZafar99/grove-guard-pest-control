## Why Your Yard Has So Many Mosquitoes

Mosquitoes need three things: water to breed, shade to rest and people or animals to bite. Many Downers Grove yards have all three.

* **Shade.** Trees, thick shrubs, ivy and tall grass keep the air cool and damp, which is where adult mosquitoes rest during the day
* **Standing water.** Mosquitoes can lay eggs in a tiny amount of water and need only about a week to grow from egg to adult
* **Wet weather.** Spring and summer rain leaves puddles and full gutters that turn into breeding spots
* **Wooded lots and low areas.** Yards that back onto trees or have low spots that stay wet after storms are more likely to have problems

***

## The Mosquitoes You May Meet

**Northern house mosquitoes.** Common around homes and active at dusk and after dark. They breed in stagnant water such as clogged gutters, birdbaths and storm drains. This is the type most linked to West Nile virus in Illinois.

**Floodwater mosquitoes.** They hatch in large numbers after heavy rain and can quickly fill a yard.

**Asian tiger mosquitoes.** Small, black with white stripes, and they bite during the day. They lay eggs in very small containers, like bottle caps and plant saucers, and can be found in parts of Illinois.

Health agencies in the Chicago area track West Nile virus in mosquitoes each summer. Most people who catch it have mild symptoms or none, but it can be serious for some. Cutting down mosquitoes around your home lowers your chance of being bitten.

## Where Mosquitoes Breed Around Homes

Check these spots every week:

* Clogged gutters and downspout extenders
* Birdbaths, pet bowls and kiddie pools
* Buckets, wheelbarrows, tarps and toys that collect water
* Flower pot saucers and planters
* Old tires and containers
* Low spots in the lawn and around drains
* Window wells and corrugated drain pipes
* Tree stumps and holes that hold rainwater
* Clogged sump pump drains and rain barrels without covers

Even a bottle cap of water is enough for some mosquitoes.

## What You Can Do

* Dump standing water at least once a week
* Clean gutters and make sure they drain
* Cover rain barrels with fine mesh
* Fill low spots in the lawn
* Trim shrubs and thin thick vegetation near the patio
* Fix window and door screens
* Use a fan on the patio, since mosquitoes are weak fliers
* Wear light colored long sleeves at dusk and use an insect repellent registered with the EPA

***

## When Mosquito Season Runs

In Illinois, mosquitoes start to appear in late spring when temperatures warm up and are heaviest from summer into early fall. Rainy weeks can cause sudden spikes. Starting treatments early in the season helps keep numbers low before they climb.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do I have so many mosquitoes when my neighbors do not?**
Your yard may have more shade, thicker plants or a source of standing water that theirs does not. Mosquitoes stay close to where they hatch, so a hidden puddle or clogged gutter on your property makes a big difference.

**How long does a mosquito treatment last?**
A treatment reduces mosquitoes for several weeks, but new mosquitoes hatch all season. That is why regular visits work better than one treatment for the whole summer.

**Will rain wash the treatment away?**
Once the treatment has dried, light rain usually does not cause a problem. Heavy rain soon after treatment can reduce results. Your technician will tell you what to watch for.

**Is mosquito treatment safe for children, pets and bees?**
Your technician follows the product label and explains where products were used and how long to keep children and pets off treated areas. Treatment is aimed at shady resting spots, not at flowers, which helps protect bees and other pollinators.

**Do citronella candles or bug zappers work?**
They have limited effect. Candles may help a little in the immediate area, and bug zappers mostly kill harmless insects. Treating resting areas and removing standing water works better.

**When should I start mosquito control?**
Late spring, before mosquito numbers build, is best. If it is already summer, you can still start and see a difference.

**Can I get rid of mosquitoes on my own?**
You can reduce them by removing standing water and trimming plants, and this helps a lot. For heavy problems, professional treatment of resting areas gives you the extra relief.
