## Termites in Illinois Homes

The termite found in this area is the Eastern subterranean termite. It lives in the soil, where it stays moist, and builds mud tubes to reach wood above ground. A single colony can have thousands of termites, and they feed on the wood inside your walls, floors and framing.

Older Downers Grove homes with basements, crawl spaces and wood close to the soil can be at risk. Termites can enter through very small cracks in a foundation, through expansion joints, or along pipes that go through the slab.

***

## Signs of Termites

Termites are often found by accident. The most common signs are:

* **Mud tubes:** thin dirt tunnels along foundation walls, joists or piers
* **Swarmers and shed wings:** winged termites indoors in spring, or small piles of wings near windows and doors
* **Damaged wood:** wood that sounds hollow when tapped, or wood that looks layered or tunneled when opened

## Termite Treatment Options

The right treatment depends on your home, where the termites are and how far the problem has spread. *(Confirm with business which methods they use and edit this section.)*

**Liquid soil treatment.** A liquid termite treatment is applied to the soil around and under your home to create a barrier. This can mean digging a trench along the foundation and, in some cases, drilling through concrete such as a patio or garage slab to reach the soil beneath. Termites that pass through the barrier are killed or carry the product back to the colony.

**Bait stations.** Stations are placed in the ground around your home. Termites feed on the bait and carry it back to the colony, which reduces and can eliminate it. Bait works more slowly, often over months, but it targets the colony itself and the stations are checked on a regular schedule.

**Wood and spot treatments.** For termites in a specific area, we may treat infested wood or a small section of the structure. This is usually used with a soil or bait treatment, not on its own.

We will explain which option fits your home and why before any work begins.

## How Long Until the Termites Are Gone?

Liquid soil treatments can start working quickly. Bait systems take longer, often several months, because the colony has to be reduced over time. Your technician will tell you what to expect for your home.

Treatment stops the termites, but it does not repair damage. If wood has been weakened, you will need a contractor. We can point out the areas that need attention.

## What Raises Termite Risk at Home

* Wood touching the soil, such as deck posts, siding or door frames
* Mulch or soil piled against the foundation
* Leaking gutters or downspouts that drain near the house
* Firewood or lumber stacked against the walls
* Damp basements and crawl spaces
* Cracks in the foundation
* Old tree stumps and roots near the home

**Ways to lower the risk**

* Keep mulch a few inches below the siding and away from the foundation
* Direct downspouts away from the house
* Fix leaks and reduce moisture in crawl spaces and basements
* Store firewood away from the home
* Keep wood off the ground where you can

## Termite Season in Downers Grove

Termites usually swarm in spring, often in April and May after warm rain. That is when many homeowners first notice them. But termites do not stop when the swarm ends. Inside a heated home, they can stay active through the winter.

If you see swarmers or shed wings at any time, call for an inspection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if I have termites?**
Look for mud tubes on foundation walls, shed wings near windows and doors, and wood that sounds hollow. Many infestations are hidden, so a professional inspection is the best way to be sure.

Application can take a day or so for most homes, depending on size and method. The termites themselves take longer to clear, from days with some liquid treatments to months with bait. *(Confirm with business.)*

**Do I need to leave my home during treatment?**
For most treatments, no. Your technician will tell you if anything needs to be avoided during or after the work. *(Confirm with business.)*

**Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage?**
Often it does not. Many policies exclude termite damage, so it is worth checking with your insurance provider.

**Can I treat termites myself?**
