## When You Should Get a Termite Inspection

* You saw winged insects or shed wings inside your home
* You found mud tubes on a wall, or wood that sounds hollow
* You are buying a home and want to know its condition
* You are selling and want to avoid surprises during the sale
* You had a leak, flood or moisture problem in the basement or crawl space
* You are about to remodel, finish a basement or build a deck
* You have an older home and have never had it checked
* It has been a few years since your last inspection

Many pest professionals suggest a check about once a year for older homes, since termites can be active for a long time before there are visible signs.

## What We Look For

**Mud tubes.** These are thin, pencil sized dirt tunnels on foundation walls, piers or joists. Termites use them to travel between the soil and wood.

**Swarmers and shed wings.** Winged termites appear in spring, often after a warm rain. They shed their wings soon after, leaving small piles near windows, doors or light fixtures.

**Damaged wood.** Termite damage runs along the grain and often leaves wood thin and hollow sounding. Probing may show soft or layered wood inside.

**Bubbling or peeling paint.** This can be a sign of moisture or hidden activity.

**Moisture problems.** Termites need moisture. Leaks, poor drainage and damp crawl spaces make a home more attractive.

## Flying Ants or Termites?

In spring, winged insects near windows are worrying. Here is how to tell the difference:

* **Antennae:** Termites have straight antennae. Ants have bent, elbowed antennae.
* **Waist:** Termites have a thick, straight body. Ants have a narrow, pinched waist.
* **Wings:** Termite wings are the same length. Ants have front wings that are longer than the back wings.

Save a few in a jar or a bag and send us a photo, or call us. We will tell you what you have.
